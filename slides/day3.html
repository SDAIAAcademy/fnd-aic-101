Day 2 review

Where should the threshold come from?

A wound-care team asks the analyst to "just use 0.5, it is the standard". What is the best reply?

Agree: 0.5 is where a probability turns into a yes
Ask what a missed long visit and a needless slot each cost
Choose the threshold with the highest accuracy
Choose the threshold that does best on the test set
Why. The threshold trades two errors; only their costs, and the base rate, say where the trade should sit.

Answer: ask for the costs. Remind the room that the owner of the decision sets the costs and approves the threshold; the analyst shows the trade-off.

Regression

For a number, measure how far off, on average

  • MAE, mean absolute error: the typical miss, in the target's units
  • RMSE, root mean squared error: like MAE, but large misses weigh more
  • RMSE is never below MAE; a big gap means a few large misses
  • Always compare with a naive baseline: predict the training mean, or the median

Five errors, by hand

Errors 3, −2, 10, −1, 0 minutes. MAE = 16 / 5 = 3.20. RMSE = √(114 / 5) = 4.77. The 10 pulls RMSE up.

Work the card on the board: absolute values 3, 2, 10, 1, 0 sum to 16; squares 9, 4, 100, 1, 0 sum to 114. These five are not the lab's rows; the lab gives five of its own to do by hand.

Learned from training rows. The mean, the spread, the median and the list of sectors are all numbers a step learns. Learn them on training rows only.

Ask why a "was missing" flag might help: a measurement may be missing for a reason, such as a client who could not stand on a scale. One-hot with handle_unknown="ignore" stops a new sector from crashing the model; Day 4 meets one.

In code

A column transformer applies each step to its columns

prep = ColumnTransformer([
    ("numbers", StandardScaler(), FEATURES),
    ("bmi", make_pipeline(SimpleImputer(strategy="median",
                                        add_indicator=True),
                          StandardScaler()), ["bmi"]),
    ("sector", OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown="ignore"), ["sector"]),
])
model = make_pipeline(prep, LogisticRegression())

Read it line by line. The pipeline is one object: fit learns every step on the rows it is given, predict applies them. That is what makes the next session's rule easy to keep.

The rule

Fit every step on training rows, inside the pipeline

1 · Split firstbefore any step learns anything
→
2 · Fit the pipelinescaling, filling, selection, model: on training rows
→
3 · Applythe fitted pipeline to validation and test
In cross-validation too. The whole pipeline goes inside the folds, so each fold prepares from its own training part.

Press once per step. scikit-learn's guide on common pitfalls makes this its first recommendation. Scaling on all rows leaks a little; choosing features on all rows can leak a lot, as the next slide shows.

A parallel example

Noise columns, selected on all rows, looked useful

  • 150 wound-care visits, 600 columns of pure noise, keep the 10 best
  • Selection on all rows, then cross-validation: AUC 0.783
  • Selection inside the pipeline: AUC 0.439, no better than chance

Why it happens

With 600 columns, some match the labels by luck. Choosing them with every label in view, then scoring on the same labels, rewards the luck.

scikit-learn's common pitfalls page shows the same effect with random data. Wide data with few rows is where it bites: sensors, genes, text. The lab has a sensor pilot with 400 columns.

Leakage

Three leaks a pipeline does not catch

Target leak

A column recorded after the outcome, often because of it: a referral, a discharge letter.

Duplicate leak

The same row on both sides of the split; the model is scored on rows it has seen.

Temporal leak

A column, or the split, that uses the future: totals over later visits, a random split of dated rows.

Not rare. Kapoor and Narayanan found leakage in 329 published papers across 17 fields.

Their survey (arXiv 2207.07048) lists eight kinds; this course keeps the four that matter most for tabular screens: preprocessing, target, duplicate and temporal. The question for each column: is it known on the day the prediction is made?

A checklist

Four questions before trusting a score

  • When is each column recorded? Read the data dictionary; anything after the prediction date goes
  • Does one column alone rank almost perfectly? Score each alone; a very good one deserves the first question
  • Are there copies? Count repeated identifiers, and test rows with a twin in training
  • Does the split match use? The screen predicts next year, so train on the past and test on the latest period

This is the checklist of Lab 3.4 and of the capstone starter in Lab 4.4. Ask the room what "known on the day" means for a wound-care screen: the wound measured at the visit is known; the dressing ordered next week is not.

Baseline first

One pipeline
